UNIT I Industrial Water and Treatment

1. Sources, Impurities and Hardness

Sources: surface water (rain – sabse pure; river, lake; sea – sabse impure, ~3.5% salts) aur underground water (springs, wells – clear par dissolved salts zyada). Impurities: dissolved (Ca/Mg ke HCO3−, Cl−, SO42−; gases CO2, O2), suspended (sand, clay – turbidity), colloidal (1–100 nm, clay, Fe(OH)3), biological (bacteria, algae).

Hardness Water ki woh property jisme soap lather nahi banata, kyunki Ca2+/Mg2+ soap ko insoluble scum bana dete hain: 2C17H35COONa + CaCl2 → (C17H35COO)2Ca↓ + 2NaCl. Temporary (carbonate): Ca/Mg bicarbonates – boiling se hatti hai: Ca(HCO3)2 → CaCO3↓ + H2O + CO2; Mg(HCO3)2 → Mg(OH)2↓ + 2CO2. Permanent (non-carbonate): Ca/Mg chlorides & sulphates – boiling se nahi jaati.
Formula Hardness as CaCO3 eq. = Mass of salt (mg/L) × 100Mol. wt of salt   (bivalent salts; CaCO3 M = 100, eq. wt 50) Mol. wt: Ca(HCO3)2 162, Mg(HCO3)2 146, CaSO4 136, CaCl2 111, MgSO4 120, MgCl2 95, MgCO3 84, Mg(NO3)2 148.  Units: 1 ppm = 1 mg/L = 0.1 °Fr = 0.07 °Cl. NaCl, KCl, Na2SO4 hardness nahi dete.

2. Water Quality and Analysis

Drinking water (BIS): pH 6.5–8.5, TDS < 500 mg/L, hardness < 200 mg/L, Cl− < 250 mg/L, pathogen-free. Boiler feed: hardness ≈ 0, no dissolved O2/CO2. Chloride – Mohr's method (AgNO3, K2CrO4 indicator, brick-red end point). DO – Winkler's method (MnSO4 + alkaline KI, I2 vs Na2S2O3).

3. Sterilization of Water

Boiling (small scale). Chlorination: Cl2 + H2O → HOCl + HCl; HOCl → HCl + [O] – HOCl bacteria ke enzymes destroy karta hai. Bleaching powder: CaOCl2 + H2O → Ca(OH)2 + Cl2. Chloramine (Cl2 + NH3 → NH2Cl + HCl) – long lasting. Break-point chlorination: itna Cl2 ki organic matter, NH3, bacteria sab oxidise ho jayein; uske baad free residual Cl2 badhta hai. Ozonation: O3 → O2 + [O] (costly, no residual). UV treatment.

4. Water Softening Processes

(a) Lime–Soda Process

Lime Ca(OH)2 + soda Na2CO3 soluble salts ko CaCO3↓ aur Mg(OH)2↓ mein badalte hain. Cold L-S: room temp, coagulant (NaAlO2, alum) chahiye, residual 50–60 ppm. Hot L-S: 80–150 °C, fast, residual 15–30 ppm.
Ca(HCO3)2 + Ca(OH)2 → 2CaCO3 + 2H2O;   Mg(HCO3)2 + 2Ca(OH)2 → 2CaCO3 + Mg(OH)2 + 2H2O;   MgCl2 + Ca(OH)2 → Mg(OH)2 + CaCl2;   CaCl2 + Na2CO3 → CaCO3 + 2NaCl;   CaSO4 + Na2CO3 → CaCO3 + Na2SO4;   CO2 + Ca(OH)2 → CaCO3 + H2O.

Formula (all in CaCO3 eq. mg/L; × Vol (L) × 100/%purity) Lime = 74100[Temp Ca2+ + 2 × Temp Mg2+ + Perm (Mg2+, Fe2+, Al3+) + CO2 + H+ (HCl/H2SO4) + HCO3− (NaHCO3)] Soda = 106100[Perm (Ca2+, Mg2+, Fe2+, Al3+) + H+ (HCl/H2SO4) − HCO3− (NaHCO3)] Yaad rakho: Mg permanent hardness ko lime aur soda dono chahiye (MgCl2 → CaCl2 → soda).

(b) Zeolite (Permutit) Process

Zeolite = hydrated sodium aluminosilicate Na2O·Al2O3·xSiO2·yH2O (Na2Ze). Na2Ze + CaCl2 → CaZe + 2NaCl; Na2Ze + Mg(HCO3)2 → MgZe + 2NaHCO3. Regeneration (10% brine): CaZe + 2NaCl → Na2Ze + CaCl2. Residual ~10 ppm, no sludge. Limitations: turbid/acidic water, Fe/Mn zeolite ko kharab karte hain; anions remove nahi hote (NaHCO3 → caustic embrittlement).

(c) Ion Exchange (Demineralization)

Cation resin (–SO3H): 2RH + Ca2+ → R2Ca + 2H+. Anion resin (–NR3+OH−): ROH + Cl− → RCl + OH−; then H+ + OH− → H2O. Regeneration: cation – dil. HCl/H2SO4; anion – dil. NaOH. Residual 0–2 ppm; best for high-pressure boilers; resins costly, turbid water nahi.

6. Conditioning of Water (Internal Treatment)

Colloidal (tannin, agar, kerosene – loose sludge); Phosphate (high pressure): 3CaCl2 + 2Na3PO4 → Ca3(PO4)2↓ + 6NaCl; Carbonate (low pressure): CaSO4 + Na2CO3 → CaCO3 + Na2SO4; Calgon: Na2[Na4(PO3)6] + 2CaSO4 → Na2[Ca2(PO3)6] (soluble) + 2Na2SO4; Sodium aluminate: NaAlO2 + 2H2O → NaOH + Al(OH)3 (flocs entrap oil/silica); electrical & radioactive conditioning; blow-down.

UNIT II Fuels and Lubricants

1. Fuels: Classification and Calorific Value

Fuel = combustible carbonaceous substance jo jalne par heat deta hai. Good fuel: high CV, moderate ignition temp, low moisture/ash, cheap, easy transport, controllable, non-polluting. Primary: solid – wood, peat, lignite, coal; liquid – crude oil; gas – natural gas. Secondary: solid – coke, charcoal; liquid – petrol, diesel, kerosene; gas – LPG, coal gas, producer/water gas, biogas.

Calorific Value Unit mass fuel ke complete combustion par total heat (kcal/kg, kJ/kg; gas kcal/m3). HCV/GCV: products room temp tak cool – steam ka latent heat included. LCV/NCV: water vapour escape. LCV = HCV − 0.09 H × 587 kcal/kg   |   Dulong: HCV = 1100[8080 C + 34500 (H − O8) + 2240 S] kcal/kg (H − O/8) = available hydrogen. Bomb calorimeter: HCV = (W + w)(t2 − t1)/x.
Example C 80%, H 5%, O 6%, S 1%: HCV = [8080×80 + 34500(5 − 0.75) + 2240×1]/100 = 7952.7 kcal/kg; LCV = 7952.7 − 0.09×5×587 = 7688.5 kcal/kg.

Fuel resources in India: Coal – Jharia, Bokaro (Jharkhand), Raniganj (WB), Talcher (Odisha), Korba (CG), Singrauli & Sohagpur (MP) – mostly bituminous, high ash; lignite – Neyveli (TN); petroleum – Digboi (Assam, oldest), Mumbai High, Ankleshwar (Gujarat), Barmer (Rajasthan) (~80% crude imported); natural gas – Mumbai High, KG basin; nuclear – U (Jaduguda), Th (Kerala monazite); renewables – hydro, solar, wind, biomass.

2. Coal: Proximate and Ultimate Analysis

Rank: wood (C 50%) → peat (57%) → lignite (67%) → bituminous (83%) → anthracite (93%); rank ↑ ⇒ C%, CV ↑, moisture, VM ↓.

Proximate (wt basis):
• Moisture: 1 g, 105–110 °C, 1 hr → loss% (CV ↓).
• Volatile matter: covered crucible, 925 ± 5 °C, 7 min (long smoky flame, CV ↓).
• Ash: open crucible, 700–750 °C (CV ↓, clinker).
• Fixed carbon = 100 − (M + VM + A) (FC ↑ ⇒ CV ↑).
Ultimate (elemental):
• C, H: burn in O2; CO2 → KOH, H2O → CaCl2.
%C = ΔKOH × 12 × 100/(44 W); %H = ΔCaCl2 × 2 × 100/(18 W)
• N (Kjeldahl): %N = 1.4 V N / W
• S (as BaSO4): %S = wt BaSO4 × 32 × 100/(233 W)
• O = 100 − (C + H + N + S + ash). C, H good; O, N, S bad.

3. Clinker Formation and Pulverized Coal

Clinker: ash (SiO2, Al2O3, Fe2O3, CaO, alkalis) high temp par fuse hokar hard glassy mass ban jaati hai jo grate par chipakti hai (low ash-fusion temp ⇒ zyada clinker) → air flow block, unburnt fuel loss. Prevention: low-ash/high fusion-temp coal, temp control, steam under grate. Pulverized coal: coal ko ~200 mesh (< 75 µm) powder bana kar hot air blast ke saath burn karna – complete combustion with less excess air, low grade coal usable, easy control, no clinker, high efficiency (thermal power plants); demerits – grinding cost, fly ash (ESP needed), dust explosion risk.

4. Carbonization and Manufacture of Coke

Carbonization = coal ko air ki absence mein heat karna → coke + gas + tar + NH3 (sirf caking/coking coal se strong coke). LTC (500–700 °C): soft semi-coke (5–15% VM, domestic), less gas but high CV (6500–9500 kcal/m3). HTC (900–1200 °C): hard porous metallurgical coke (1–3% VM), more gas, lower CV (5400–6000 kcal/m3).
Beehive oven: dome brick chamber, coal partly burns inside, by-products waste, 3–4 days, obsolete. Otto–Hoffman by-product oven: narrow silica chambers, regenerative heating (flue gas se checker bricks garam, phir air/gas preheat), 11–18 hr, water quenching; by-products recovered – tar, NH3 [(NH4)2SO4], naphthalene, benzene, H2S. Good metallurgical coke: low ash/S/P, porous, strong, low reactivity, uniform size.

5. Petroleum, Cracking and Cracked Gasoline

Refining fractions: LPG (C1–C4), petrol (C5–C8, 40–120 °C), naphtha, kerosene (C10–C16, 180–250 °C), diesel (C15–C18, 250–320 °C), heavy oil (C17–C30, 320–400 °C → lube oil, wax), residue → bitumen.
Cracking = bade hydrocarbons ko heat/catalyst se chhote low-boiling molecules mein todna (more petrol): C10H22 → C5H12 + C5H10. Thermal: liquid-phase 475–530 °C, 100 kg/cm2; vapour-phase 600–650 °C, low pressure (ON ~65–70). Catalytic (Al2O3–SiO2/zeolite, 300–450 °C, 1–5 atm): fixed-bed or fluid-bed (FCC) with continuous catalyst regeneration – better yield, ON 80–100, less S and gum.
Cracked gasoline: olefins + branched alkanes + aromatics ⇒ high octane (better anti-knock than straight-run); par olefins storage mein polymerise hokar gum banate hain → inhibitors. Mercaptans hatane ko Doctor sweetening: 2RSH + Na2PbO2 → (RS)2Pb + 2NaOH; (RS)2Pb + S → RSSR + PbS. Reforming (Pt) ON aur badhata hai.

6. Knocking, Octane and Cetane Number

Knocking (SI engine): spark ke baad bacha hua fuel-air mixture flame front aane se pehle hi self-ignite ho jaata hai → shock wave, metallic sound, power loss, damage. Tendency: n-paraffins > branched paraffins > olefins > cycloparaffins > aromatics. Octane number = % iso-octane (2,2,4-trimethylpentane, ON 100) in iso-octane + n-heptane (ON 0) mixture jo same knocking de. Diesel knock (CI): ignition delay se fuel jama hokar ek saath jalta hai; straight-chain best. Cetane number = % n-cetane (C16H34, CN 100) in cetane + α-methylnaphthalene (CN 0) mixture. High ON ⇒ low CN.
Anti-knock: TEL (C2H5)4Pb + ethylene dibromide (Pb → volatile PbBr2); toxic, catalytic converter poison → now unleaded with MTBE, ethanol (E10/E20), aromatics. Cetane improvers: ethyl/amyl nitrate, acetone peroxide.

2. EMF, Cell Potential and Nernst Equation

Galvanic cell chemical → electrical energy: anode (oxidation, −), cathode (reduction, +), salt bridge. EMF = zero current par electrodes ka potential difference. Reference SHE (E° = 0). Daniell: Zn | Zn2+ ‖ Cu2+ | Cu, E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode = 0.34 − (−0.76) = 1.10 V.

Nernst Equation E = E° − RTnF ln Q = E° − 0.0591n log Q (298 K) ;   electrode: E = E° + 0.0591n log [Mn+] ;   log K = nE°0.0591

Applications: electrode potential at any conc.; pH (H-electrode E = −0.0591 pH, glass electrode); equilibrium constant; concentration cell E = (0.0591/n) log (C2/C1); Ksp; potentiometric titrations; corrosion prediction.

Example Daniell, [Zn2+] = 0.1, [Cu2+] = 0.01 M: E = 1.10 − (0.0591/2) log 10 = 1.070 V. ΔG° = −2×96500×1.10 = −212.3 kJ; log K = 2.20/0.0591 = 37.2 ⇒ K ≈ 1.7 × 1037.

4. Corrosion: Theories and Mechanism

Corrosion = metal ka environment se chemical/electrochemical reaction se gradual destruction (rusting Fe2O3·xH2O) – reverse of metallurgy.
(a) Dry (chemical) corrosion – gases se direct attack, high temp. Oxidation: 2M → 2Mn+ + 2ne−; nO2 + 4ne− → 2nO2−. Oxide film: stable non-porous (Al, Cr) – protective; unstable (Ag, Au, Pt) – no corrosion; volatile (MoO3) & porous – continuous. Pilling–Bedworth ratio = vol. oxide / vol. metal: < 1 porous, non-protective (Li, Na, K, Mg); ≥ 1 protective (Al, Cr). Also Cl2, H2S, SO2 attack, liquid metal corrosion.
(b) Wet (electrochemical) corrosion – electrolyte ki presence mein anodic & cathodic areas. Anode: Fe → Fe2+ + 2e−. Cathode: H2 evolution (acidic, no O2): 2H+ + 2e− → H2; O2 absorption (neutral, aerated): O2 + 2H2O + 4e− → 4OH−; Fe2+ + 2OH− → Fe(OH)2; 4Fe(OH)2 + O2 + 2H2O → 4Fe(OH)3 → rust. Types: galvanic (dissimilar metals – active one anode), differential aeration (kam O2 wala part anode – waterline, under dirt), pitting, crevice, stress corrosion, intergranular.

DryNo moisture; direct chemical; uniform; slow; product at attack site

5. Factors Affecting Rate of Corrosion

Metal: position in galvanic series (Mg, Zn, Al, Cd, steel, Pb, Sn, brass, Cu, Ni, Ag, Ti, Au, Pt – active → noble; zyada difference = fast); small anode + large cathode = intense; impurity, stressed parts, nature of oxide film, solubility/volatility of products, over-voltage. Environment: temperature ↑, humidity (critical ~60–80%), low pH, impurities (SO2, H2S, Cl− near sea), conductance, O2 concentration difference.

6. Protection: Cathodic, Anodic and Other Measures

Cathodic protection – structure ko cathode banana. (i) Sacrificial anode: Mg/Zn/Al block jodo; woh corrode hota hai, structure bachta hai (pipelines, ship hulls, tanks). (ii) Impressed current: DC source ka −ve structure se, +ve inert anode (graphite, scrap iron in gypsum backfill) se (long pipelines, tanks).
Anodic protection: potentiostat se anodic current dekar metal ko passive region mein rakhna (protective oxide). Only active–passive metals – stainless steel, Ti, steel in H2SO4 tanks.

Other measures: proper design (avoid dissimilar metal contact, large anode/small cathode, no crevices, drainage); material selection (pure metals, stainless steel Cr ≥ 12%); modifying environment – deaeration, dehumidification, inhibitors: anodic (chromates, phosphates), cathodic (amines, As/Sb salts), vapour-phase (dicyclohexylammonium nitrite); protective coatings.

UNIT IV Cement, Refractories and High Polymers

1. Cement: Types and Manufacture of Portland Cement

Types: natural, puzzolana (PPC – fly ash), slag, OPC (33/43/53 grade), rapid hardening (more C3S), low heat (dams), sulphate resisting (C3A < 5%), white (Fe-free), high alumina, quick setting. Portland cement composition: CaO 60–67, SiO2 17–25, Al2O3 3–8, Fe2O3 0.5–6, MgO 0.1–4, SO3 1–3%.
Manufacture: calcareous (limestone) + argillaceous (clay) → mixing by dry (powder, less fuel) or wet process (slurry, better mixing) → rotary kiln (inclined rotating steel cylinder, flame at lower end) → clinker → cooled, ground with 2–3% gypsum. Kiln zones: drying (~400 °C, water out) → calcination (700–1000 °C, CaCO3 → CaO + CO2) → clinkering (1400–1500 °C: 2CaO + SiO2 → C2S; 3CaO + SiO2 → C3S; 3CaO + Al2O3 → C3A; 4CaO + Al2O3 + Fe2O3 → C4AF).

2. Setting and Hardening of Cement

Reactions Setting = plasticity loss; hardening = strength gain (hydration + gel formation + crystallisation).
Initial set (1 day): 3CaO·Al2O3 + 6H2O → 3CaO·Al2O3·6H2O + 880 kJ/kg;   C4AF + 7H2O → 3CaO·Al2O3·6H2O + CaO·Fe2O3·H2O
Gypsum (retarder): 3CaO·Al2O3 + 3CaSO4·2H2O + 26H2O → 3CaO·Al2O3·3CaSO4·32H2O (ettringite, stops flash set)
Hardening (1–7 d): 2(3CaO·SiO2) + 6H2O → 3CaO·2SiO2·3H2O (tobermorite gel) + 3Ca(OH)2
Hardening (7–28 d): 2(2CaO·SiO2) + 4H2O → 3CaO·2SiO2·3H2O + Ca(OH)2

Properties of OPC: initial setting ≥ 30 min, final ≤ 600 min; soundness (Le-Chatelier ≤ 10 mm); fineness ≥ 225 m2/kg; 28-day strength 33/43/53 MPa; heat of hydration. Uses: concrete, RCC, mortar, plaster, dams, bridges, roads, pipes.

3. Refractories

Refractory = inorganic material jo high temperature par bina soften/deform hue load, abrasion aur slag attack sah sake – furnace, kiln, converter linings. Classification: acidic – silica, fireclay (acid slag resist; for coke ovens, glass furnace roofs); basic – magnesite (MgO), dolomite (basic slag; open hearth, LD converter); neutral – chromite, graphite, SiC, zirconia (both; separating layer, crucibles). By refractoriness: normal 1580–1780 °C, high 1780–2000 °C, super > 2000 °C.
Properties: refractoriness (softening temp – Seger cone/PCE test), refractoriness under load (RUL, 3.5 kg/cm2), porosity (low = strong, slag resistant), thermal spalling resistance, dimensional stability, suitable thermal conductivity, low expansion, chemical inertness, crushing strength. Uses: blast furnace, coke ovens, cement/glass kilns, boilers, crucibles, rocket nozzles, nuclear reactors.

4. High Polymers: Classification and Mechanism

Polymer = monomers ke covalent bonding se bani macromolecule; degree of polymerization = no. of repeating units; functionality = reactive sites (2 → linear, 3 → cross-linked). Classification: origin – natural (rubber, cellulose), semi-synthetic, synthetic; structure – linear (HDPE), branched (LDPE), cross-linked (bakelite); mode – addition vs condensation; homo- vs co-polymer; thermoplastic vs thermosetting; tacticity – iso-, syndio-, atactic.

Free radical (chain) addition (initiator benzoyl peroxide):
Initiation: (C6H5COO)2 → 2C6H5· + 2CO2; R· + CH2=CH2 → RCH2CH2·
Propagation: RCH2CH2· + nCH2=CH2 → R(CH2CH2)nCH2CH2·
Termination: combination or disproportionation.
Ionic: cationic (BF3/H2O – isobutylene), anionic (n-BuLi, KNH2 – acrylonitrile). Coordination: Ziegler–Natta TiCl4 + Al(C2H5)3 → linear, stereoregular. Condensation (step-growth): bifunctional monomers, small molecule (H2O, HCl) eliminated – nylon, bakelite.

6. Plastics: Preparation, Engineering Properties and Uses

Thermoplastics (PE, PVC, PMMA, nylon): linear, heat se soft – reshape & recycle possible. Thermosets (bakelite, epoxy, UF): 3-D cross-linked, heating par permanently hard, insoluble, not recyclable.
Preparation (compounding & moulding): resin (binder) + plasticizer (DOP) + filler (wood flour, mica) + pigment + stabilizer (Pb salts for PVC) + catalyst/accelerator + lubricant; moulded by compression (thermosets), injection (thermoplastics), transfer, extrusion (pipes), blow moulding (bottles), calendering (sheets).
Engineering properties: low density, high strength/weight, corrosion & chemical resistance, electrical/thermal insulation, easy fabrication, transparency, low cost; limitations – low heat resistance, creep, flammable, UV degradation, non-biodegradable. Uses: auto & aircraft parts, electrical fittings, pipes, packaging, medical devices.

7. Natural and Synthetic Rubbers; Compounding and Vulcanization

Natural rubber = cis-1,4-polyisoprene from latex of Hevea brasiliensis, coagulated by acetic/formic acid (gutta-percha = trans form). Raw rubber drawbacks: sticky in summer, brittle in winter, low tensile strength, swells in solvents, oxidises, high water absorption.
Compounding: vulcanizing agent (S), accelerators (MBT, TMTD) + activator (ZnO), antioxidants, reinforcing filler (carbon black), softeners/plasticizers, colours. Vulcanization (Goodyear 1839): rubber + 3–5% S at 100–140 °C → allylic positions par –S–S– cross-links → tensile strength ~10×, elasticity ↑, non-sticky, wide temp range (−40 to 100 °C), solvent & abrasion resistance ↑. 30–50% S → ebonite (hard).

8. Silicones and Adhesives

Silicones = (R2SiO)n, backbone –Si–O–Si– with organic groups on Si. Rochow: 2CH3Cl + Si → (CH3)2SiCl2 (Cu, 300 °C); hydrolysis → (CH3)2Si(OH)2 + 2HCl; condensation → –[Si(CH3)2O]n–. R3SiCl chain stopper, RSiCl3 cross-linker. Properties: thermally stable (−90 to 250 °C), water repellent, chemically & physiologically inert, good insulator. Uses: oils (lubricant, hydraulic fluid), greases, rubbers (gaskets, implants), resins (insulating varnish, water-proofing).
Adhesives = substances jo surfaces ko surface attachment se jodte hain; adhesion by mechanical interlocking, adsorption, chemical bonding, diffusion. Natural: starch, animal glue, casein, shellac. Synthetic: epoxy (bisphenol-A + epichlorohydrin, amine hardener – metal, glass), phenol/urea-formaldehyde (plywood), PVA (wood), cyanoacrylate (super glue), polyurethane, neoprene-based. Factors: clean rough surface, wetting, curing. Uses: plywood, packaging, aircraft/auto assembly, electronics, footwear.

UNIT V Environmental Chemistry and Instrumental Techniques

A1. Pollution and Air Pollution

Pollution = air/water/soil mein undesirable change jo living beings ke liye harmful ho. Pollutants: primary (SO2, CO) & secondary (O3, PAN, H2SO4). Causes: population, industrialisation, vehicles, fossil fuels, deforestation, chemicals, poor waste disposal.
Air pollutants: CO (incomplete combustion → carboxyhaemoglobin, O2 transport block); SOx (coal → respiratory, acid rain); NOx (vehicles → smog, acid rain); hydrocarbons (carcinogenic); PM10/PM2.5 (lungs, asthma); Pb, CFCs. Types: natural vs anthropogenic, indoor vs outdoor, gaseous vs particulate. Classical smog (smoke + fog + SO2, reducing). Photochemical smog (oxidizing): NO2 + hν → NO + O; O + O2 → O3; O3 + HC → HCHO, PAN (eye irritation). Acid rain (pH < 5.6): 2SO2 + O2 + 2H2O → 2H2SO4; 4NO2 + O2 + 2H2O → 4HNO3 – marble (Taj Mahal) damage.

A2. Green House Effect and Ozone Layer

Greenhouse effect: sun ki short-wave radiation earth ko garam karti hai; earth IR re-emit karti hai jise CO2, CH4, N2O, CFCs, O3, H2O vapour absorb karke wapas bhejte hain. Natural effect earth ko ~15 °C rakhta hai (warna −18 °C); excess GHG → global warming – ice melting, sea level rise, extreme weather, crop loss. Control: renewable energy, afforestation, efficiency, Kyoto (1997), Paris (2015).
Ozone layer (stratosphere ~15–35 km, ~300 DU) harmful UV-B/UV-C (200–315 nm) absorb karti hai → skin cancer, cataract, immune & crop damage se bachav. Formation: O2 + hν (λ < 242 nm) → 2O; O + O2 + M → O3 + M. Depletion by CFCs: CF2Cl2 + hν → ·CF2Cl + Cl·; Cl· + O3 → ClO· + O2; ClO· + O → Cl· + O2 (one Cl destroys ~105 O3); NO + O3 → NO2 + O2. Antarctic ozone hole; Montreal Protocol (1987).

A3. Control of Air Pollution

Particulates: settling chamber, cyclone separator, bag filter, wet scrubber, electrostatic precipitator (particles charged by corona discharge, collected on plates, > 99% efficient). Gases: absorption (SO2 + CaCO3 lime slurry scrubbing), adsorption (activated carbon), incineration, catalytic converter (Pt–Pd–Rh: 2CO + O2 → 2CO2; 2NO + 2CO → N2 + 2CO2). Others: CNG/LPG, low-S fuel, BS-VI, tall chimneys, green belts, Air Act 1981.

A4. Water Pollution: Sources and Prevention

Sources: domestic sewage (organics, pathogens, detergents); industrial effluents – heavy metals (Hg → Minamata, Cd → itai-itai, As, Pb), dyes, acids; agricultural runoff – fertilizers → eutrophication, pesticides (DDT biomagnification); thermal pollution; oil spills; radioactive waste. Indicators: BOD (O2 used by microbes, 5 days, 20 °C; clean < 5 ppm), COD (K2Cr2O7 oxidation; COD > BOD), DO (> 4–6 ppm good).
Prevention/treatment: primary (screening, grit, sedimentation) → secondary (biological: activated sludge, trickling filter, oxidation pond) → tertiary (chemical precipitation, ion exchange, RO, disinfection); ETPs in industries, controlled fertilizer use, no dumping in rivers, Water Act 1974.

A5. Soil/Land and Radioactive Pollution

Soil pollution causes: solid & plastic waste, industrial/hazardous & e-waste, excess fertilizers & pesticides, mining, acid rain, sewage sludge, deforestation (erosion), salinity. Control: 3R (reduce, reuse, recycle), waste segregation, composting/vermicomposting, sanitary landfill, incineration, bio-fertilizers/bio-pesticides, organic farming, afforestation, bioremediation.
Radioactive pollution sources: natural (cosmic rays, radon, 40K, U/Th rocks); man-made (nuclear power & waste, weapon tests, U mining, medical X-rays, accidents – Chernobyl 1986, Fukushima 2011). Effects: somatic (burns, leukaemia, cancer) & genetic (mutations); 90Sr → bones, 131I → thyroid, 137Cs → muscles. Control: safe disposal (vitrification + deep geological storage), shielding (Pb, concrete), time–distance–shielding, leak-proof reactors, monitoring (film badges/dosimeters), dose limits (AERB/ICRP).

B1. Spectroscopy and Lambert–Beer's Law

Spectroscopy = matter aur EM radiation ke interaction (absorption/emission) ka study. E = hν = hc/λ = hcν̄. Regions: UV 200–400 nm, visible 400–800 nm (electronic transitions); IR 4000–400 cm−1 (vibrations); microwave (rotations); radio (nuclear spin – NMR).

Lambert–Beer's Law Monochromatic light ki absorbance path length (Lambert) aur concentration (Beer) ke proportional: A = log I0I = ε c l ;   T = I/I0 ;   A = 2 − log(%T) ε = molar absorptivity (L mol−1 cm−1). Valid only for dilute solutions (< 0.01 M), monochromatic light, no association/dissociation, scattering or fluorescence. Ex: A = 0.50, ε = 5000, l = 1 cm ⇒ c = 1 × 10−4 M; %T = 10 ⇒ A = 1.

B2. UV–Visible Spectroscopy

Principle: UV/visible absorb karke valence electrons bonding/non-bonding se antibonding orbital mein jaate hain; energy order σ→σ* > n→σ* > π→π* > n→π*. Chromophore (C=C, C=O, NO2, N=N) – absorption ka group; auxochrome (–OH, –NH2) – λmax/intensity badhata hai. Shifts: bathochromic (red), hypsochromic (blue), hyperchromic, hypochromic. Conjugation ↑ ⇒ λmax ↑ (butadiene 217 nm). Instrument: H2/D2 lamp (UV), tungsten (visible) → monochromator → quartz cell → photomultiplier → recorder. Applications: conjugation/chromophore detection, quantitative analysis, impurity detection (benzene in alcohol), cis-trans isomers, kinetics.

B4. NMR Spectroscopy

Principle: spin I ≠ 0 nuclei (1H, 13C; I = ½) external field B0 mein do energy states (α aligned, β opposed) mein split hote hain; RF absorb karke spin flip – resonance ν = γB0/2π. Electron cloud shielding → upfield; electronegative groups deshielding → downfield. Chemical shift δ = (νsample − νTMS)/νspectrometer × 106 ppm; reference TMS Si(CH3)4, δ = 0 (12 equivalent, highly shielded H; inert, volatile). δ: CH3 0.9, CH–C=O 2–2.5, OCH3 3.3–4, aromatic 6.5–8, CHO 9–10, COOH 10–12. Signals = types of equivalent H; area ∝ no. of H; splitting (n + 1) rule. Ethanol: CH3 triplet, CH2 quartet, OH singlet (3:2:1). Instrument: magnet, RF transmitter, sweep coil, receiver; solvent CDCl3/CCl4. Applications: structure elucidation, isomers, purity, tautomerism, MRI.

B5. Chromatography and Gas Chromatography

Chromatography (Tswett): mixture components ko stationary aur mobile phase ke beech different adsorption/partition ke basis par separate karna. Types: column, paper, TLC, GC, HPLC, ion-exchange. Rf = distance by solute / distance by solvent front. Uses: separation, purification, identification (amino acids, dyes, drugs, forensics).

Gas chromatography: mobile phase = inert carrier gas (He, N2, Ar); stationary = solid (GSC) or liquid on support (GLC, partition). Carrier gas → heated injector → column in thermostated oven → detector (TCD, FID, ECD) → recorder → chromatogram. Retention time → identity; peak area → amount. Applications: petroleum, essential oils, air pollutants, pesticide residues, blood alcohol, drugs. Only volatile, thermally stable samples.

B6. Colorimetry

Coloured solution ki concentration uski visible absorbance se (Beer–Lambert). Colorimeter: tungsten lamp → filter (complementary colour) → cuvette → photocell → galvanometer. Standards se calibration curve (A vs c, straight line), unknown ki A se c. Colourless ions pehle coloured complex mein. Applications: Fe (thiocyanate/phenanthroline), Cu (NH3 blue), NH3 (Nessler's), nitrite, phosphate, Mn (as MnO4−), glucose/haemoglobin in blood.
