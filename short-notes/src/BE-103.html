UNIT I Application of Linguistic Ability

1. Articles (a, an, the)

Articles noun se pehle aate hain aur batate hain ki noun definite (specific) hai ya indefinite (koi bhi). a/an = Indefinite article, the = Definite article.

a — consonant sound se pehle: a book, a university (yoo-), a one-way road (wun-), a European.

an — vowel sound se pehle: an apple, an hour (silent h), an honest man, an MBA (em-), an X-ray.

a/an — pehli baar mention, profession, rate: She is an engineer. 60 km an hour.

the — already mentioned/specific: I bought a laptop. The laptop is fast.

the — unique objects, superlatives, ordinals: the sun, the best student, the first chapter.

the — rivers, seas, mountain ranges, newspapers, holy books, plural countries: the Ganga, the Himalayas, the Hindu, the Gita, the USA.

the — instruments, inventions, whole class: She plays the guitar. The computer has changed our lives.

No article — proper nouns, languages, meals, games, abstract nouns (general sense): He speaks English. We play cricket. Honesty is the best policy.

Common Mistake "He is a honest boy" ✗ → "He is an honest boy" ✓. "She is an university topper" ✗ → "She is a university topper" ✓. Rule: sound decide karta hai, letter nahi.

2. Prepositions

Preposition woh word hai jo noun/pronoun ka relation (time, place, direction, cause) sentence ke dusre word se batata hai: in, on, at, by, for, since, with, into, between, among, etc.

TypeRule → Example

Since / Forsince = point of time: I have lived here since 2020. · for = period: I have lived here for five years.
Placeat point (at the gate) · in enclosed area (in the room) · on surface (on the table) · between two, among more than two
Motioninto movement inside, in position, to direction: He jumped into the river. He went to Delhi.
Fixedgood at, interested in, afraid of, depend on, agree with (a person), agree to (a proposal), comply with, superior to, senior to
Common Mistake "He is senior than me" ✗ → "senior to me" ✓. "Discuss about the problem" ✗ → "Discuss the problem" ✓. "I have been ill since two days" ✗ → "for two days" ✓.

3. Subject–Verb Agreement (Concord)

Basic rule: Singular subject → singular verb, plural subject → plural verb. Verb ko subject ke number aur person ke saath match karna chahiye.

1. Two subjects + and → plural. Ram and Shyam are friends. (But same person/idea → singular: Bread and butter is my breakfast.)

2. Either…or / Neither…nor / or → verb nearest subject se agree. Neither the teacher nor the students were present.

3. Each, every, everyone, nobody, either, neither → singular. Each of the boys has a pen.

4. as well as, along with, with, together with → first subject se agree. The manager, along with his staff, is coming.

5. One of + plural noun → singular verb. One of my friends lives in Pune.

6. Collective noun → unit ho to singular. The committee has taken a decision. Members alag-alag act karein to plural: The jury were divided.

7. Amount/distance/time as a unit → singular. Ten kilometres is a long distance.

8. Words that look plural (news, physics, mathematics) → singular. The news is true. Scissors, trousers, spectacles → plural. My spectacles are new.

9. The number of → singular; A number of → plural. The number of students is 60. A number of students are absent.

10. Relative pronoun (who/which/that) → antecedent se agree. He is one of those engineers who work hard.

4. Active and Passive Voice

Active: subject action karta hai (The engineer designed the bridge.). Passive: action ka receiver subject ban jata hai (The bridge was designed by the engineer.). Technical/scientific writing mein passive zyada use hota hai kyunki focus process/result par hota hai, doer par nahi.

FormulaPassive = Object (as subject) + be (tense ke hisaab se) + V3 (past participle) + by + Subject (as object)

5. Sentence Structure

(a) Basic patterns (S = Subject, V = Verb, O = Object, C = Complement, A = Adverbial)

S + V: The machine stopped.

S + V + O: The student solved the problem.

S + V + C: Copper is a good conductor.

S + V + A: The meeting is in the seminar hall.

S + V + IO + DO: The teacher gave us a project.

S + V + O + C: They elected him captain.

S + V + O + A: She put the file on the desk.

(b) Types by structure

  • Simple sentence — sirf ek independent clause (one finite verb): The engine overheated due to low coolant.
  • Compound sentence — do ya zyada independent clauses, coordinating conjunction (and, but, or, so, yet — FANBOYS) se jude: The coolant was low, so the engine overheated.
  • Complex sentence — ek main clause + ek ya zyada subordinate clauses (because, although, when, if, which, that): The engine overheated because the coolant was low.
  • Compound-complex — dono ka mix: The coolant was low, so the engine overheated when we climbed the hill.

Types by function: Declarative (statement), Interrogative (question), Imperative (command/request), Exclamatory (strong feeling) — What a brilliant design it is!

6. Reported Speech — Direct and Indirect

Direct speech mein speaker ke exact words inverted commas mein hote hain. Indirect (reported) speech mein uska meaning apne words mein report karte hain, bina inverted commas.

Rule 1: Tense change (jab reporting verb past mein ho)

  • Simple present → Simple past (is → was)
  • Present continuous → Past continuous
  • Present perfect → Past perfect
  • Simple past → Past perfect
  • will → would, shall → should, can → could, may → might
  • must → had to (obligation)
  • Universal truth / habitual fact → tense nahi badalta
  • Reporting verb present/future → tense nahi badalta

Rule 2: Pronoun & Rule 3: Time/place words

Pronoun: 1st person → subject of reporting verb ke according; 2nd person → object of reporting verb ke according; 3rd person → no change (SON rule: Subject–Object–No change).

now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day / the following day, yesterday → the previous day / the day before, here → there, this → that, these → those, ago → before, next week → the following week.

Rule 4: Sentence-type ke hisaab se

TypeDirectIndirect
AssertiveHe said, "I am busy today."He said that he was busy that day.
TruthThe teacher said, "The earth revolves around the sun."The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun.
Yes/No questionShe said to me, "Have you finished the project?"She asked me if/whether I had finished the project.
Wh- questionHe said to me, "Where do you live?"He asked me where I lived.

Exam Tip Question ko indirect banane par question mark hata do aur word order statement jaisa karo (subject + verb): "asked me where I lived" — where did I live ✗. Reporting verb change: said → told (with object), asked, ordered, requested, advised, exclaimed.

UNIT II Communication Skills

1. Meaning of Communication

Definition Communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, thoughts and feelings between two or more persons through a common system of symbols (words, signs, gestures) so that mutual understanding is achieved. Word Latin "communis" (= common) se aaya hai.

Matlab sirf bolna kaafi nahi — jab tak receiver message ko sender ke intended meaning mein na samjhe aur feedback na de, communication complete nahi hota.

2. Process of Communication

  1. Sender (Encoder) — jiske mann mein idea aata hai, communication shuru karta hai.
  2. Encoding — idea ko symbols (words, pictures, gestures) mein convert karna.
  3. Message — encoded content jo bhejna hai.
  4. Channel/Medium — message kis raste se jayega: face-to-face, phone, e-mail, letter, notice.
  5. Receiver — jisko message milta hai.
  6. Decoding — receiver symbols ko interpret karke meaning nikalta hai.
  7. Feedback — receiver ka response; isse sender ko pata chalta hai ki message sahi samjha gaya ya nahi.
  8. Noise — koi bhi disturbance (physical, semantic, psychological) jo message ko distort kare.

3. Significance of Effective Communication

  • Organisation mein planning, coordination aur control ka base.
  • Sahi aur timely decision making.
  • Misunderstanding aur conflict kam karta hai.
  • Employees ka motivation aur morale badhata hai.
  • Teamwork aur good human relations.
  • Customers/clients ke saath public relations aur goodwill.
  • Leadership aur personality development.
  • Time, money aur effort ki bachat (rework kam).

UNIT III Developing Writing Skills

1. Comprehension Skills

Reading comprehension = passage ko padhkar uska meaning, main idea, details aur writer ka purpose samajhna aur questions ka answer dena.

Reading techniques

  • Skimming — fast reading to get the gist/main idea (title, first & last para, topic sentences).
  • Scanning — specific information (date, name, figure) dhundhna, jaise timetable mein train ka time.
  • Intensive reading — detailed, careful reading for full understanding (textbook, agreement).
  • Extensive reading — pleasure/general knowledge ke liye bade texts (novels, magazines).

Steps to answer a comprehension passage

  1. Questions pehle padho, phir passage.
  2. Passage 2 baar padho: pehle skim, phir carefully.
  3. Answers apne words mein, complete sentences; lines copy mat karo.
  4. Vocabulary: context se meaning guess karo; same part of speech mein answer.
  5. Answer passage ke facts par based ho, apni opinion nahi.
  6. Title question: central idea batane wala short title.

2. Essay Writing (Technical and Current Topics)

Essay ek topic par organised, coherent prose composition hai. Types: descriptive, narrative, expository, argumentative, reflective.

  • Title — clear and relevant. Introduction (1 para) — hook (quote, fact, question), background, thesis statement.
  • Body (3–4 paras) — har para mein ek idea: topic sentence + explanation + example/data; linkers (moreover, however, therefore, for instance).
  • Conclusion (1 para) — summary + suggestion/future outlook; naya point nahi.

Sample topics: Artificial Intelligence: Boon or Bane; Electric Vehicles and the Future of Transport; Digital India; Cyber Security; Renewable Energy; Role of Engineers in Nation Building; Climate Change; 5G Technology; Startups in India.

Sample Intro "Artificial Intelligence (AI) is no longer a concept of science fiction; it is a part of our daily lives, from voice assistants to medical diagnosis. It offers great benefits, but it also raises serious concerns about jobs, privacy and ethics."

3. Precis Writing

Definition A precis is a clear, concise and logical summary of a passage, written in the writer's own words, which retains all the essential ideas of the original. Normally length = one-third of the original.

Steps

  1. Passage 2–3 baar padho, central theme samjho.
  2. Main points underline/note karo.
  3. Words count karo; target = ⅓ (e.g., 300 → ~100 words).
  4. Rough draft apne words mein, logical order mein.
  5. Compare with original — koi key point chhuta na ho; phir fair copy.
  6. Suitable title do; end mein word count likho.

Rules (Do's and Don'ts)

  • Third person aur past tense / indirect speech use karo.
  • Examples, illustrations, repetitions, quotations, figures of speech hatao.
  • Apni opinion/comment add mat karo.
  • Abbreviations aur note-form nahi — complete sentences.
  • Phrases ko one-word substitutes se replace karo ("a person who cannot read or write" → "illiterate").

4. Noting and Drafting

Government/official offices mein kisi file par decision lene ka process. Noting aur Drafting dono official procedure ke part hain.

Definition Noting: written remarks recorded on a file (on the "note sheet") to facilitate the disposal of a case — summary of facts, relevant rules/precedents and suggested course of action. Drafting: preparing the preliminary version (draft) of an official communication — letter, memo, circular, notice, order — for approval by a competent authority before it is issued.

Features of a good note

  • Brief, objective, factual, impersonal (third person).
  • Problem/receipt ka summary, relevant rules/precedents.
  • Clear suggestion/recommendation.
  • Paragraphs numbered; signed with name, designation, date.

Features of a good draft

  • Approved note ke decision ke according.
  • Correct format, reference number, date, subject.
  • Clear, concise, polite official language.
  • "Draft for approval" likha jata hai; approval ke baad fair copy issue hoti hai.

5. Technical Definition

Technical definition kisi technical term ka precise, unambiguous meaning batata hai. Three types:

  • Informal (parenthetical) definition — synonym ya short phrase brackets/commas mein: The alternator (an AC generator) charges the battery.
  • Formal (sentence) definition — three parts: Term = Class (genus) + Distinguishing features (differentia).
  • Extended (amplified) definition — formal definition ko explain karna through examples, etymology, comparison/contrast, analogy, cause-effect, illustration/diagram, principle of working.
TermClassDistinguishing features
A thermometeris an instrumentthat measures temperature.
A transformeris a static electrical devicethat transfers AC power from one circuit to another at the same frequency by electromagnetic induction.
A compileris a programthat translates source code written in a high-level language into machine code.
Common Mistake (1) Circular definition: "A generator is a device that generates electricity" ✗ (same word repeat). (2) Class bahut broad ("A lathe is a thing…") ✗. (3) "is when / is where" use mat karo: "Corrosion is when metal rusts" ✗.

6. Technical Description

Technical description kisi object/mechanism (e.g., a bicycle pump) ya process (e.g., how a refrigerator works) ka precise, objective, detailed account hai, taaki reader use samajh sake ya bana/use kar sake. Language: objective, impersonal, precise measurements (with units), labelled diagram.

Description of an object/mechanism

  1. Introduction: definition, purpose/function, overall appearance (size, shape, material), main parts list.
  2. Part-by-part description: har part ka shape, size, material, position, function (spatial order).
  3. Conclusion: how parts work together, one complete cycle of operation, uses.

Description of a process

  1. Introduction: what the process is, purpose, equipment/materials required.
  2. Steps in chronological order, mostly in passive voice, present tense.
  3. Conclusion: result/output, precautions.
Sample "A fuse is a safety device which protects an electric circuit from excessive current. It consists of a thin wire of low melting point, made of an alloy of tin and lead, enclosed in a porcelain holder. When the current exceeds the rated value, the wire heats up and melts, and the circuit is broken."

UNIT IV Business Correspondence

1. Introduction to Business and Official Letters

Business letters firms, companies aur customers ke beech trade-related communication ke liye (enquiry, order, complaint). Official letters government offices/institutions ke beech ya unko likhe jaate hain (application, permission, circulars). Dono formal, permanent written record aur legal evidence hote hain. Qualities: 7 C's, proper layout, You-attitude, correct salutation/close, neat presentation, promptness.

2. Layout of a Business Letter

Parts (in order)

  1. Heading / Letterhead — sender's name, address, phone, e-mail
  2. Reference number (Ref. No.) and Date (5 October 2026)
  3. Inside address — receiver's designation, company, address
  4. Attention line (optional) — Kind attn.: Purchase Manager
  5. Salutation — Dear Sir/Madam, / Dear Mr Sharma,
  6. Subject line — Subject: Enquiry about…
  7. Body — opening, main, closing paragraphs
  8. Complimentary close — Yours faithfully, / Yours sincerely,
  9. Signature — signature, name, designation
  10. Enclosures (Encl.), cc (copy to)

Formats (styles)

  • Full block — sab kuch left margin par, no indentation (most popular).
  • Modified block — date, close, signature right side; paras left aligned.
  • Semi-block — modified block + indented paragraphs.

4. E-mail Writing

  • To / Cc / Bcc — Cc: visible copy; Bcc: hidden copy
  • Subject — short, specific
  • Salutation → Body (purpose in first line, short paras) → Closing (Regards) → Signature block; attachment ka mention body mein
  • Netiquette: professional e-mail ID, no ALL CAPS (shouting), no emojis/slang, proofread, check attachment, "Reply all" carefully, reply within 24 hours.

UNIT V Report Writing

1. Report — Meaning and Features of a Good Report

Definition A report is a formal, factual and organised document prepared for a specific purpose and audience, which presents information, findings, analysis, conclusions and (often) recommendations on a particular subject.
  • Accuracy — facts, figures, data sahi aur verified.
  • Objectivity — unbiased, impersonal (third person, passive).
  • Clarity & Precision — simple, specific language.
  • Conciseness — sirf relevant matter.
  • Logical structure — headings, sub-headings, numbering.
  • Completeness — saare aspects covered.
  • Visual aids — tables, graphs, charts.
  • Timeliness — time par submit.
  • Reader-oriented — audience ke need ke hisaab se.
  • Proper format and documentation (references, appendices).

Types: Formal/Informal; Long/Short; Informational (only facts) / Analytical (facts + analysis + recommendations); Periodic/Routine (progress report) / Special (trouble report); Oral/Written.

2. Structure of a Formal Report

SectionElements and contents
Prefatory (Front) matterTitle page (title, submitted to/by, date) · Letter of transmittal (covering letter) · Preface/Acknowledgement · Table of contents, list of figures/tables · Abstract / Executive summary (purpose, findings, recommendations in short — busy reader ke liye)
Main bodyIntroduction (background, purpose, scope, methodology, limitations) → Discussion/Findings (data, analysis, tables/graphs) → Conclusions (what the findings mean) → Recommendations (suggested action)
Supplementary (End) matterReferences/Bibliography · Appendices (supporting data) · Glossary · Index
Exam Tip Short formal report (memo/letter style) ke liye: To, From, Date, Subject → Introduction → Findings → Conclusions → Recommendations → Signature. Exam mein yahi format likho.

3. Report of Trouble (Trouble Report / Incident Report)

Kisi accident, breakdown, malfunction ya problem ki report jo higher authority ko turant bheji jati hai taaki corrective action liya ja sake. Facts clear, time-specific, objective; kisi par blame lagana nahi.

Headings yaad rakho: What happened (description, date, time, place) → Cause → Effect/Damage → Immediate action taken → Recommendations to prevent recurrence.

To: The Plant Manager      Date: 5 October 2026
From: Shift Engineer, Boiler Section
Subject: Breakdown of Boiler No. 2
1. Trouble: At 10:30 a.m., Boiler No. 2 tripped
   due to a sudden drop in feed-water pressure.
2. Cause: The feed-water pump motor burnt out
   because of overloading.
3. Effect: Steam supply to Unit B was stopped for
   three hours. No one was injured.
4. Action taken: The standby pump was started and
   normal operation was restored at 1:30 p.m.
5. Recommendations: Replace the motor, install an
   overload relay and carry out monthly preventive
   maintenance of the pumps.
                     Sd/- (Name), Shift Engineer

4. Laboratory Report

Laboratory mein kiye gaye experiment ka record — taaki koi bhi use repeat karke result verify kar sake. Language: passive voice, past tense (procedure), impersonal.

Structure: (1) Title, Experiment No., Date → (2) Aim/Objective → (3) Apparatus → (4) Theory/Principle (with formula) → (5) Circuit/Diagram → (6) Procedure (numbered steps) → (7) Observations (table) → (8) Calculations → (9) Result → (10) Precautions/Sources of error → (11) Conclusion/Discussion.

Example — Aim: To verify Ohm's law. Procedure: "The circuit was connected as shown in the diagram. The voltage was increased in steps of 1 V, and the corresponding current was noted." Result: "The ratio V/I was found to be constant; hence, Ohm's law is verified."

5. Progress Report

Ongoing project/work ki status batane wali periodic report (weekly, monthly, quarterly). Management ko pata chalta hai ki work schedule ke according chal raha hai ya nahi, aur kya problems hain.

Headings: Introduction (project, period covered) → Work completed → Work in progress → Problems faced → Work planned for next period → Overall appraisal (on/behind schedule, cost).

To: The Project Head       Date: 5 October 2026
From: Site Engineer, Hostel Block-C
Subject: Progress report for September 2026
1. Introduction: Construction began on 1 July 2026;
   completion is scheduled for 31 March 2027.
2. Work completed: foundation and ground-floor

3. Problems faced: Work was delayed by eight days
   due to heavy rain and a shortage of cement.
4. Next month: completing the first-floor slab.
5. Appraisal: The project is one week behind
   schedule but will be completed on time with
   an additional shift.          Sd/- Site Engineer

6. Slogan Writing

Slogan = short, catchy, memorable phrase jo ek idea/message ko promote kare (social awareness, product, campaign). Features: short (3–8 words), catchy, rhyme/alliteration, positive, easy to remember, clear message.

Save water: "Save water, save life." / "Every drop counts."

Road safety: "Speed thrills but kills."

Environment: "Go green, keep it clean."

Cleanliness: "A clean India is a healthy India."

Energy: "Switch off — save energy, save money."

Cyber safety: "Think before you click."

7. Advertising

Advertisement = paid, non-personal communication by an identified sponsor to promote a product, service or idea through media (newspaper, TV, radio, internet, hoardings). Types: Classified (small, text-only, in columns — situations vacant, for sale, to let, matrimonial) aur Display/Commercial (big, design, pictures, slogans).

AIDA model

  • Attention — catchy headline/visual
  • Interest — features of the product
  • Desire — benefits, offers, discount
  • Action — "Call now", contact/address

8. Book Review

Book review ek critical evaluation hai — book ka summary + strengths/weaknesses + recommendation. Sirf summary nahi, judgement bhi zaroori hai.

Structure: (1) Bibliographic details — title, author, publisher, year, pages, price, genre → (2) Introduction — author and theme → (3) Summary — brief outline (poora plot reveal mat karo) → (4) Critical evaluation — style, language, presentation, strengths and weaknesses → (5) Conclusion & Recommendation — who should read it, overall rating.

Sample "Wings of Fire (Universities Press, 1999) is the autobiography of Dr A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, written with Arun Tiwari. It traces his journey from a small town, Rameswaram, to becoming one of India's leading scientists. The book describes his work on the SLV-3 and on India's missile programme. Written in simple and inspiring language, it shows how hard work and faith can overcome every obstacle. Although some technical sections may seem lengthy to general readers, the book is highly recommended for every engineering student."
